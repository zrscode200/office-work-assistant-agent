The problem

The same fact ends up in five places

Meeting takeaways in a doc. The decision in a chat thread. Status in a spreadsheet. Follow-ups in a todo app. The reasoning in someone's head. Each copy drifts, and nobody knows which one is current.

Why this exists02 / 16

What you get

Three things change on day one

One home, no copies

Every fact lives in one note, project or follow-up. Status updates and summaries are built from it when you ask, so there is nothing to keep in sync.

Ask, don't dig

"Brief me for Thursday" or "What changed since Monday?" gives you the current picture in seconds, with sources, instead of an afternoon of compiling.

Share on purpose

Everything is private until you publish it. Your team sees exactly what you chose to share, and nothing else.

The basics

It keeps three kinds of things

Projects

What a project is for, how it is going, and where that understanding comes from.

Notes

Your thinking, meeting context, evidence and the reasons behind decisions. A quick note and its developed version are the same note.

Follow-ups

Who does what by when. A simple local item, or a link to a Jira issue.

Status updates, briefings and meeting prep are assembled from these three whenever you ask.

Privacy

Private by default

Your notes and follow-ups are yours. Link a private note to a team project and it appears in your view of that project. The team still cannot see it.

A briefing for yourself includes your private notes. A briefing for the team leaves them out, so it is safe to send onward.

Privacy09 / 16

Team

Sharing with the team

  1. Pull the latest. Your copy of the shared folder catches up with what teammates published.
  2. Make your change. Update a project, add a shared note, track a team follow-up. It stays on your computer for now.
  3. Publish. The assistant shows you exactly which records change and where they go. You say yes, and only those are shared.

Local until you publish. The assistant always tells you which state a change is in.

Team10 / 16

Team

When two people change the same thing

It happens

You and a teammate both update the Atlas project before either of you publishes.

Merged, part by part

Their change to the purpose and your change to the status both survive. Nothing is overwritten.

You decide the rest

Only when you both changed the very same part does the assistant show both versions and ask which to keep.

Nothing is lost and nothing is forced. The assistant never rewrites what the team already has.

Your assistant14 / 16

Start

Getting started, and what it never does on its own

Three steps

  1. Get a folder set up. Whoever looks after the toolkit installs it into a folder of yours, for the assistant you use. Your team's shared folder goes inside it.
  2. Say who you are. Your name goes on what you save. The tour can set it for you.
  3. Open your assistant there and say I'm new here. Help me get started.

Never without asking you

  • Publish or pull team changes.
  • Contact Jira or send a message anywhere.
  • Copy your private notes into something shared.
  • Overwrite a teammate's change.

Always

  • Keeps a history of who changed what.
  • Shows you what will be shared before sharing it.
  • Keeps passwords and tokens out of your files.
